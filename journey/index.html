2005 – 2012

Industrial mechanic at Daimler

My working life started in manufacturing. I trained as an industrial mechanic at the Daimler truck plant in Mannheim and earned my university entrance qualification alongside it. I then worked in engine machining, CNC tool management and the foundry.

Production taught me what I still rely on: processes have to be reproducible, and a small mistake early in the chain becomes an expensive one at the end.

2012 – 2015

University

I went on to study in Aschaffenburg: first industrial engineering, then business administration and law.

2015 – 2021

Losing my sight and starting again

In 2015 I lost my sight. The following years were about medical rehabilitation and learning to work without seeing: training in assistive technology, Braille, and new routines for everyday tasks.

From 2020 to 2021 I took part in a vocational rehabilitation programme at QuikStep in Durmersheim. I learned C and the fundamentals of computer science, and I learned to work entirely without visuals: screen reader, keyboard shortcuts, audio output and Braille. That year showed me that programming is text, and that text is something I can work with at full speed.

2021 – 2024

Apprenticeship at the German Cancer Research Center

I completed an inclusive apprenticeship as IT specialist for application development (Fachinformatiker Anwendungsentwicklung) at the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) in Heidelberg, with vocational school at the Hubert-Sternberg-Schule in Wiesloch.

  • Federated Information Systems, first year: distributed systems, medical informatics, Java, APIs, Docker, Linux, Git, databases and Scrum.
  • Cancer Information Service: web development with PHP and JavaScript on an online counselling platform.
  • Federated Information Systems, final year: Rust, and the department's Samply.beam, a communication component for strictly regulated clinical networks. My final project was a room booking system with a REST API, an ORM, Docker and a dynamic frontend.

2024 – 2025

Developer and administrator

After the apprenticeship I stayed in the department as a developer. I built secret-sync, a Rust component that automates application and user enrolment against Authentik and Keycloak, and secured the pseudonymisation service Mainzelliste with OAuth.

I was also responsible for server administration and internal services (Confluence, GitLab, Authentik) and for managing research partner groups. Along the way I wrote a Java generator for synthetic FHIR test data, API documentation with Swagger UI, and a cross-platform version of the room booking software with Tauri.

2025 – 2026

Technical lead

I became technical lead for two data platforms in cancer research:

  • ITCC Federated Data Hub – international integration of paediatric cancer data across clinical sites. Federated ingestion of FHIR and genetic data, pseudonymised central storage with S3 and Apache Iceberg, deployment on Kubernetes.
  • LeMeDart – a data hub with partners across Germany: central data ingestion from REDCap, pseudonymisation and storage.

I was responsible for architecture, backend services, data processing and deployment. I also designed reusable architecture models for data warehouse projects across departments, and secured the Java-based Samply Exporter with several Spring Security filter chains and group-based authorisation via OIDC.

Since 2026

DevOps and my own platform

Today my focus is on the whole path from code to running system: containers, CI/CD with GitHub and GitLab, Kubernetes (CKAD certified), Helm, Azure and S3 storage.

I put all of it together in my own project, the Accessible Job Manager: an application management platform for blind and partially sighted people, built with Spring Boot and Angular, deployed with Docker on Azure Container Apps, and accessible by design rather than as an afterthought.

How I work

I work in the terminal with the Linux screen reader Fenrir, in NeoVim (AstroNvim) and with Claude Code. Architecture diagrams are usually the least accessible part of a project, so I write mine as text in PlantUML: ERDs, class diagrams, process flows and network views.

Working this way makes me careful about things that are easy to skip: clear documentation, reproducible builds and interfaces that work without a mouse. Those habits help every team, not only blind colleagues.
